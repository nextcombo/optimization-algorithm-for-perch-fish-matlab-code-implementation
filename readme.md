💥1 Overview
The stingray belongs to the family Stingrayidae, with its body being transparent and tubular. Its tissues are highly similar to those of hydroids. Its movement is also very similar to that of hydroids, where water is pumped into the body as propulsion force for forward movement.
Biological research on this type of creature is at an early milestone mainly because their living environment is extremely difficult to access, and it is indeed challenging to keep them in laboratory settings. The focus of this paper is on their swarm behavior. In deep seas, stingrays often form a chain called a shark school. The main reason for this behavior is not yet clear, but some researchers believe that doing so allows for better coordination and foraging through rapid changes.
There are almost no mathematical models for swarm behavior and stingray populations in the literature. Moreover, there are no mathematical models for salp swarms used to solve optimization problems, while swarms, ants, and fish schools have been widely used for modeling and solving optimization problems. Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) mimics salps to solve optimization problems.
